Welcome to Q-Spatial 2026

The 2nd ACM SIGSPATIAL International Workshop on Quantum Computing and Quantum-Inspired Technologies for Spatial Data Systems and Applications

Workshop Overview

Quantum computing and other quantum-driven technologies have been a field of technology investment for decades. With promising potential, significant investments from many governmental and industrial parties are poured into winning the race. The SIGSPATIAL community is building a capacity in that promising technology field. This workshop aims to bring together SIGSPATIAL community members interested in exploring quantum technologies in spatial systems and applications. It will also invite non-SIGSPATIAL members who are experts in the field of quantum technologies to enrich the community and foster learning and collaboration. The workshop will be open to all levels of expertise, allowing participants to contribute or learn.

Workshop Mission and Goals

The high-level philosophy of organizing this workshop is engaging, motivating, encouraging, and helping interested SIGSPATIAL community members to explore spatial problems that could take advantage of quantum computing. This mission breaks down into the following goals:

  1. Community building: building a sub-community within ACM SIGSPATIAL that is interested in exploring research and applications using quantum technologies.
  2. Research innovations: pushing high-quality quantum-driven research into the mainstream SIGSPATIAL conference and other relevant journals and research venues.
  3. Community vision: building a community vision towards a rich and concrete agenda for SIGSPATIAL contributions to the quantum technologies field.

Call for Papers

2nd ACM SIGSPATIAL International Workshop on Quantum Computing and Quantum-Inspired Technologies for Spatial Data Systems and Applications (Q-Spatial 2026)

Quantum computing and other quantum-driven technologies, such as quantum communication and quantum sensing, have been a focus of investment for decades across diverse scientific and engineering communities, including major professional societies. Recent advances and breakthroughs from academia and industry have direct relevance to spatial applications, such as GPS-free navigation using quantum navigators. Several countries worldwide are investing hundreds of millions of dollars in quantum technologies through public and private partnerships. We strongly believe it is time for the SIGSPATIAL community to build capacity in this promising field of technology.

Q-Spatial 2026 brings together members of the research community interested in exploring quantum technologies in spatial systems and applications, whether experts in the field or those seeking to learn about them at different levels of seniority and expertise. The workshop series is designed to benefit all levels of expertise, whether contributing or learning, with sessions tailored to audiences with varying levels of experience in quantum technology.

Topics of Interest

We welcome submissions at the intersection of quantum computing / quantum-inspired technologies and spatial data systems and applications, including (but not limited to):

  • Quantum computing models for spatial data: the circuit model and quantum annealing
  • Quantum sensing and quantum communication for spatial applications
  • Quantum and quantum-inspired algorithms for spatial optimization (routing, transportation, facility location selection)
  • Quantum approaches to spatial and geospatial database problems
  • GPS-free navigation and quantum navigators
  • Quantum machine learning for spatial and geospatial data
  • Quantum use cases, experiments, and benchmarks on real spatial problems
  • Tooling, Python libraries, and simulators for spatial quantum modeling
  • Educational approaches and tutorials for spatial quantum computing

Submission Types

The workshop welcomes submissions of different types. All page limits below exclude references and appendices, and submissions should follow the current ACM SIGSPATIAL formatting guidelines. All submissions are peer-reviewed by the program committee.

Research Papers

Categories: novel research, quantum use cases, quantum experiments, vision papers, and review papers.

  • Novel research: Original, previously unpublished work that advances the quantum and spatial frontier, including new models, algorithms, theory, system designs, or methods.
  • Quantum use cases: Submissions that identify and articulate spatial problems or application domains where quantum or quantum-inspired computing could add value. A working implementation is not required; motivating scenarios, problem formulations, and proposed quantum or hybrid pipelines are all in scope.
  • Quantum experiments: Empirical studies that run spatial workloads on quantum hardware, simulators, or annealers. Benchmarks, feasibility and scalability studies, reproducibility reports, and informative negative results are all encouraged.
  • Vision papers: Forward-looking submissions that outline promising directions, open challenges, or roadmaps for quantum technologies in spatial computing.
  • Review papers: Surveys, systematization-of-knowledge, and tutorial-style overviews that organize and synthesize existing work at the intersection of quantum technologies and spatial data systems and applications.
Type Page limit
Full papers up to 10 pages
Short papers up to 4 pages
Extended abstracts up to 2 pages

Any of the above contribution kinds may be submitted at the length that best fits its maturity: full papers for complete, evaluated work; short papers for focused or preliminary results; and extended abstracts for early-stage ideas, positions, or demonstrations.

Educational Papers

Tutorials, enlightening talks, and lightning talks.

  • Tutorials: Lecture-style or hands-on sessions that teach quantum concepts, computing models (the circuit model and quantum annealing), tools, or programming libraries to the SIGSPATIAL audience. Any level, from introductory to advanced, and any format, from slides to live-coding notebooks, is welcome.
  • Enlightening talks: Longer talks that illuminate a concept, share a perspective or experience, or connect quantum technologies to spatial research. These may be conceptual, experiential, or visionary, and need not present new technical results.
  • Lightning talks: Very short talks on early ideas, tools, datasets, open questions, or calls for collaboration.
Type Page limit
Tutorials and similar educational submissions up to 6 pages
Enlightening talks (extended abstract) up to 2 pages
Lightning talks (extended abstract) up to 2 pages
